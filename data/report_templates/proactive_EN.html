Methods and Limitations

Genomic DNA obtained from the submitted sample is enriched for targeted regions using a hybridization-based capture protocol and sequenced using Illumina technology. Unless otherwise indicated, targeted regions are sequenced to ≥50× depth or supplemented with additional analysis as needed. Reads are aligned to the GRCh38 reference assembly.

Hereditary health screening (proactive panel). Sequence variants are identified and interpreted in genes associated with hereditary cancer predisposition, cardiovascular disease, and metabolic or other adult-onset conditions (see Genes Evaluated appendix). Analysis focuses on coding sequence of clinically relevant transcripts, adjacent intronic splice regions, and selected non-coding regions included in the panel design. Small variants are classified using ACMG/AMP guidelines. Copy-number changes at targeted exons may be assessed using read-depth–based algorithms compared with internal reference samples. Variants classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic and confirmed by laboratory review are reported; variants of uncertain significance and benign or likely benign variants are not included unless otherwise noted.

Pharmacogenomics (PGx). Pharmacogenomic analysis is performed using PharmCAT, which matches variant calls to published star-allele definitions and assigns predicted phenotypes and activity scores based on CPIC and DPWG clinical guidelines. Additional pharmacogenes and selected tag variants are genotyped against an extended PGx panel. Drug–gene interaction summaries follow published CPIC/DPWG recommendations when actionable diplotypes are identified. Where included on this report, APOE ε2/ε3/ε4 tag-SNP genotypes are provided for supplemental Alzheimer disease and lipid-related risk context and are not a stand-alone diagnostic test.

Sex-chromosome markers may be evaluated for sample quality control; discrepancies with reported sex may be noted per laboratory policy. This report reflects clinically reviewed results as of the report date. Population allele frequencies may be cited from gnomAD, dbSNP, and other public resources (rsID identifiers). PMID references denote supporting literature.

Limitations. This assay does not detect all disease-causing variants. Promoters, deep intronic regions, untranslated regions, and most non-coding sequence outside the designed capture are not fully interrogated. Structural variants, large rearrangements, triplet-repeat expansions, gene conversions, and variants in repetitive or segmentally duplicated regions may not be detected or fully resolved. Mosaicism, phasing ambiguity, and mapping uncertainty may limit interpretation. PGx results depend on current allele-definition catalogs; rare or novel haplotypes may be missed, and star-allele assignments may change as evidence evolves. PGx findings do not account for drug–drug interactions, organ function, or other non-genetic factors. Negative hereditary and PGx results do not eliminate future disease risk or guarantee normal drug response. Results should be interpreted by a qualified healthcare professional in the context of the full clinical picture. This report reflects analysis of extracted genomic DNA; in rare circumstances the sample may not represent the patient’s constitutional genome (e.g., post-transplant, transfusion, or malignancy-related findings).

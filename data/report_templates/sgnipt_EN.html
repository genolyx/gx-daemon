Methods and Limitations

Genomic DNA from maternal plasma is enriched for targeted genomic regions using a hybridization-based capture protocol and sequenced on an Illumina sequencing platform. Sequencing reads are aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh38) using standard alignment tools. Variant calling is performed using validated bioinformatics pipelines designed for low-fraction allele detection consistent with cell-free fetal DNA analysis.

Fetal fraction is estimated from informative SNP allele fractions in the maternal plasma. A minimum fetal fraction of approximately 4% is generally required for reliable variant detection; samples with lower fetal fraction or failed estimation are flagged accordingly. Fetal variant origin (fetal-specific, maternal-inherited, or ambiguous) is inferred based on expected allele fractions given the estimated fetal fraction and maternal genotype at each locus.

Uniparental disomy (UPD) analysis is performed by evaluating the allele balance of informative SNPs on imprinted chromosomes. This analysis requires a minimum number of informative heterozygous or homozygous maternal SNPs per chromosome; results are flagged as insufficient when coverage does not meet this threshold.

This assay targets coding regions and specific clinically relevant intronic positions of the evaluated gene(s). Variants in non-coding regions, deep intronic variants, large structural rearrangements, and copy number variations outside the targeted scope of this assay may not be detected. Mosaic variants at very low allele fractions may be below the limit of detection. This test is intended as a prenatal risk assessment tool and does not replace confirmatory diagnostic testing. Positive results should be confirmed by invasive prenatal diagnosis (CVS or amniocentesis) followed by postnatal genetic testing of the neonate.
